## The Two Architectures: Self-Hosted vs. SaaS

Every directory platform falls into one of two camps, and the choice between them shapes everything that follows.

**Self-hosted** means you install WordPress on hosting you control, add a directory plugin, and your data, design, customer relationships, and SEO authority all live on infrastructure you own. You handle updates and security (or pay a managed host to do it). You pay a flat annual fee — typically $0 to $250 per year for the plugin — and that fee doesn't compound. Over five years, a serious self-hosted setup might cost you $1,145 total.

**SaaS** means you pay a monthly subscription to a vendor like Brilliant Directories or eDirectory, and they handle hosting, security, updates, and support. Your site runs on their servers using their templates. Setup is faster. The trade-off is that the monthly fee compounds forever (a $145/month plan is $1,740/year, every year, for the life of the directory), your data lives on someone else's servers, and you're at the mercy of their pricing decisions, terms-of-service changes, or eventual shutdown.

The math gets brutal over time. Five years on a $145/month SaaS plan is $8,700. Five years on a self-hosted unlimited-sites plan is around $1,145. That gap is real money — money that could go into marketing the directory instead of feeding it to a platform.

But SaaS isn't the wrong choice for everyone. If you have zero technical tolerance, no interest in managing a server, and want to launch a working directory this weekend, SaaS removes a lot of friction. The question is whether you're willing to pay for that friction removal indefinitely.

- **Multilingual support** for directories serving multiple language markets

- **Payment processing** with recurring billing and failed-payment recovery

## Common Questions People Ask Before They Pick

**Is there any free directory software worth using?** Yes, with caveats. GeoDirectory's free core is genuinely capable — single and multi-location directories, custom post types, Google Maps, front-end submissions, and SEO-friendly archives all come free. Softr has a permanently free tier for basic use (1 app, 10 users). The free options work for prototypes and small projects. They don't work for serious monetized directories — you'll hit ceilings fast.

**How much does directory software typically cost?** Basic solutions run $20–50/month. Professional platforms run $50–150/month. Enterprise tools run $150+/month or custom pricing. Self-hosted options typically run $0–250/year flat. The total cost of ownership over five years is where the real difference shows up — SaaS compounds, self-hosted doesn't.

**Can I switch platforms later?** Technically yes. Practically, it's painful. Data export tools rarely preserve the full structure of your directory, and SEO authority built on one platform's URL structure doesn't transfer cleanly to another. This is why the "own vs. rent" decision matters so much upfront — migrating a directory business is a project, not a task.

**Do I need technical skills to launch a directory?** Depends on the platform. Brilliant Directories, eDirectory, and the no-code tools are built for non-technical operators. GeoDirectory and other WordPress plugins assume basic WordPress competence. None of them require you to write code, but the WordPress route assumes you're comfortable with hosting, plugin management, and occasional troubleshooting.

**What's the single biggest mistake people make when picking directory software?** Optimizing for setup speed instead of long-term ownership. The platform that gets you live in a weekend can cost you $8,700 over five years and leave you with no data portability. The platform that takes a week to set up can cost you $1,145 over five years and leave you with a fully owned asset. Speed is real. So is the bill.
